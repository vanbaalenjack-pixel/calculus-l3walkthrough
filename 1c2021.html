Question

A curve has the equation

\[ y=(2x+3)e^{x^2}. \]

Find the \(x\)-coordinate(s) of any stationary point(s) on the curve.

You must use calculus and show any derivatives that you need to find when solving this problem.

First walkthrough idea

Focus to try first

Differentiate first, then use \(e^{x^2}>0\) to simplify the stationary-point equation.

Step 1

Differentiate the product

Use the product rule on \((2x+3)e^{x^2}\).

Show the first step’s working
\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=2e^{x^2}+(2x+3)e^{x^2}(2x) \] \[ \frac{dy}{dx}=e^{x^2}(4x^2+6x+2) \]

Common mistake to avoid

Do not stop after differentiating the outside function; include the derivative of the inside function as a factor.
