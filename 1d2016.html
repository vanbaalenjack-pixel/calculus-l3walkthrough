Question

The tangents to the curve

\[y=\frac14(x-2)^2\]

at points P and Q are perpendicular.

Q is the point \((6,4)\).

What is the \(x\)-coordinate of point P?

You must use calculus and show any derivatives that you need to find when solving this problem.

First walkthrough idea

Focus to try first

Find the gradient at Q, take its negative reciprocal, then use the derivative again to locate P.

Step 1

Differentiate the parabola

The derivative gives the tangent gradient at any point on the curve.

Show the first step’s working
\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=\frac14\cdot2(x-2)=\frac{x-2}{2}. \]

Common mistake to avoid

Use the derivative for the gradient and the original curve for the point before forming the tangent equation.
