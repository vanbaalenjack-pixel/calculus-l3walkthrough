Question

What is the remainder when

\[ 2x^3-3x^2+4x+3 \]

is divided by \(x-2\)?

First walkthrough idea

Focus to try first

Use the Remainder Theorem: division by \(x-2\) gives remainder \(f(2)\).

Step 1

Name the polynomial and divisor root

Write the dividend as \(f(x)\). The divisor \(x-2\) is zero at \(x=2\).

Show the first step’s working

Let

\[ f(x)=2x^3-3x^2+4x+3. \]

By the Remainder Theorem, dividing \(f(x)\) by \(x-2\) leaves the constant remainder \(f(2)\).
